When is the best time to move in Canada?

By Steven Paiva  ·  August 22, 2026  ·  7 min read

Short answer: Late September to mid-October. Temperatures are still manageable, moving rates drop 15 to 25 percent below the summer peak, and movers have open dates. For Ontario specifically, avoid the last three days of any month — leases end then and everything is booked. Mid-month, mid-week moves get the best rates year-round.

The Canadian moving calendar

Canada has two moving seasons that most people don't think about: the peak (late May through August) and the off-peak (November through March). The shoulder seasons, April to May and September to October, offer the best combination of reasonable weather and lower costs.

Season by season

Best overall

Fall — September and October

Temperatures in Ontario sit between 10°C and 22°C through most of October. The summer rush is over. School started in September, which pulls a large portion of family movers off the market. Moving companies that were booked 6 weeks out in July have dates available within 2 to 3 weeks.

Rates drop 15 to 25 percent below peak summer prices. Daylight runs from roughly 7am to 7pm through October, giving a full working day. Rain is possible but nowhere near as frequent as spring.

Book by mid-September for an October move. First two weeks of October are the easiest to schedule.

Good, with caveats

Spring — April and May

April and early May offer reasonable weather and lower costs than summer, but spring in Ontario is unpredictable. Rain is common. March mud season bleeds into April in rural areas. May picks up fast and rates start climbing toward summer levels by mid-month.

April is the better of the two months if you can manage the rain risk. Plastic totes handle wet conditions without softening or collapsing, which makes a rainy April move less stressful than one with cardboard boxes.

Peak season

Summer — June, July, August

Summer is the most popular moving season in Canada, and it costs the most. Moving companies in the GTA book out 6 to 8 weeks in advance by late June. Rates sit at their highest. Condo elevator slots fill weeks before your date.

July is the hardest month to move. The combination of heat, humidity, and peak demand means carrying heavy furniture up stairs in 35°C weather with a truck you booked two months ago. August eases slightly as families settle before school starts.

If summer is your only option, book early and avoid moving at midday. Schedule delivery and pickup for morning.

Hard but cheap

Winter — November through March

Winter moves cost the least. January and February see rates 30 to 40 percent below summer peak, and movers often have dates on a week's notice. For anyone with scheduling flexibility, winter is the cheapest time to move in Canada.

The trade-offs are real. Ice on steps and walkways is a hazard for movers and furniture. Daylight in January runs roughly 8am to 5pm. Electronics and musical instruments need insulation against rapid temperature changes when moving between a heated house and a cold truck.

Salt paths the night before. Use moving blankets on wood furniture to protect against moisture. Book a morning start to use peak daylight and avoid the coldest pre-dawn hours.

Mid-month and mid-week cost less

Ontario leases run on monthly cycles. The last three days of any month concentrate a large share of all moves onto the same dates. Movers charge peak rates for month-end moves, condo elevators book up weeks in advance, and street parking for a moving truck becomes a competition.

Moving between the 10th and 20th of a month costs less than moving on the 28th, 29th, or 30th regardless of season. A Tuesday or Wednesday move costs less than a Saturday.

In Ontario, most tenants give 60 days notice. If your lease ends on August 31 and you have flexibility, give notice to end September 30 instead. Your move shifts to mid-September, costs drop, and movers are available.

What changes in a Canadian winter move

Most of the challenges are logistics, not impossibilities.

  • Ice: Salt all paths and stairs the night before. Lay rubber-backed mats at doorways.
  • Electronics: Pack TVs, computers, and instruments last and carry them in the cab of the truck, not the cargo hold. Temperature swings cause condensation inside screens and speaker cones.
  • Furniture: Wood expands and contracts with humidity shifts. Protect finished surfaces with moving blankets, not plastic wrap, which traps moisture against the wood.
  • Daylight: Start by 9am. A January move that starts at noon runs out of light before the truck is empty.
  • Plumbing: If your new home has been unoccupied, confirm the heat has been running before move day. Pipes in unheated houses freeze.

Tote rentals and Canadian weather

Plastic totes handle every Canadian season better than cardboard. Rain, snow, and humidity do not soften them. A cardboard box left on a porch in light rain collapses. A plastic tote sits there until you pick it up.

In winter, totes also stack more stably on icy surfaces than mismatched cardboard boxes, and the hard lids protect contents from snow landing on an open truck.

Questions

What is the cheapest month to move in Ontario?
January and February are the cheapest months to hire movers in Ontario. Demand is at its lowest and movers often have open dates on short notice. The trade-off is cold temperatures, ice, and shorter daylight hours. For most people, October offers the better balance: rates are 15 to 25 percent lower than summer and the weather is still manageable.
Is it a bad idea to move in Canadian winter?
Not if you plan for it. Ice on stairs and walkways is the main hazard. Salting paths the night before, using moving blankets on furniture, and packing electronics in the truck cab rather than the cargo hold makes a winter move workable. Book a morning start and you have 8 to 9 hours of usable daylight even in January.
Why is the end of the month a bad time to move?
Most Ontario leases end on the last day of the month. That concentrates a large portion of all moves into the last three days of any given month. Movers charge premium rates, elevator booking windows at condos fill up weeks in advance, and parking a moving truck is harder to secure. Mid-month moves avoid all of this.
Does weather affect plastic moving tote rentals?
No. Plastic totes are waterproof and hold up in rain, snow, and humidity without softening or collapsing. This is one of the advantages of tote rentals for Canadian moves — a cardboard box left in light rain becomes unusable. A plastic tote does not.
When should I book movers in the GTA for a summer move?
Book at least 6 to 8 weeks in advance for a summer move in the GTA, especially for late June and the first two weeks of July. August is slightly easier to book than July. For a September or October move, 3 to 4 weeks is usually enough. For winter moves, 1 to 2 weeks is often sufficient.
