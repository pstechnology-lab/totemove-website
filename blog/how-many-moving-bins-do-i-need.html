How many moving bins do I need? A room-by-room guide

By Steven, Founder  ·  July 2026  ·  Brampton, ON

The most common moving mistake isn't bad packing tape or a truck that's too small. It's running out of containers halfway through pack day, whether that's cardboard boxes from a second store run or a tote package that's one size too small.

This guide gives you a reliable count by home size and packing style, then breaks it down room by room so you know where the surprises come from. If you're renting totes from ToteMove, the numbers at the bottom map directly to our packages.

These ranges assume you're packing everything, including the kitchen and at least some storage. "Light" means you declutter regularly and have minimal basement or garage contents. "Heavy" means you have closets packed to the ceiling, a basement storage room, or a garage full of gear.

Room-by-room breakdown

The averages below are for a typical room with normal accumulation. Go higher if a room is particularly packed; go lower if it's sparse or mostly furniture with minimal loose items.

KITCHEN
8–14 totes
The most underestimated room. Counter appliances alone use 3–5 totes. A full pantry adds 3–4 more. Dishware and glassware need to be packed loosely; figure 4–6 totes for that alone.
LIVING ROOM
5–8 totes
Books pack dense; limit to about 20–25 lbs per tote. Electronics, cables, and accessories: 1–2 totes. Decorative items and framed art: 2–3 totes depending on scale.
PRIMARY BEDROOM
7–10 totes
Clothes fill more than expected when packed folded: 3–5 totes for a shared closet. Bedding and linens: 2–3 totes (bulky but light). Nightstands, lamps, personal items: 1–2 totes.
ADDITIONAL BEDROOMS
5–8 totes each
Kids' rooms run toward the high end; toys have odd shapes and don't pack as efficiently as clothes or books.
HOME OFFICE
3–6 totes
Books and files are heavy; pack small. Equipment, cables, and accessories: 2–3 totes. Papers and documents: 1–2 totes.
BATHROOMS
1–2 totes each
Usually fast to pack. Medicine cabinets and under-sink storage can add a tote each if they're stocked.
BASEMENT STORAGE
5–20+ totes
The most variable room in any home. Seasonal bins, holiday decorations, tools, sports equipment, and boxes from previous moves all live here. Don't estimate this one blind; open the door and actually count the shelves.
GARAGE
Count separately
Load large items (bikes, lawnmower, shelving) directly into the truck. Smaller garage items, like hand tools, hardware, and sports gear, typically fill 5–10 totes if packed.

The weight rule matters more than the count

ToteMove totes hold up to 60 lbs each. Unlike cardboard, the bottom won't give out, but that doesn't mean you should pack to capacity every time. A 60-lb tote is hard work on stairs.

A practical rule by what you're packing:

  • Dense items (books, dishes, tools, canned goods): fill to about 50% of tote volume
  • Medium items (clothes, small appliances, electronics): fill to about 70%
  • Light bulky items (pillows, bedding, stuffed animals): fill completely; they'll be light even when the tote looks full

This has a direct effect on how many totes you need. If you're packing books at 50% capacity, you're using roughly twice as many totes for books as you would for clothes. That's expected, which is why the kitchen and office tend to use more totes than their physical size suggests.

Five things people consistently undercount

After enough moves, the surprises stop being surprising. These are the rooms and items that push people over their estimated count:

  • The kitchen. It's almost always the room that takes longest to pack and uses more containers than any other. Small appliances alone: toaster, coffee maker, air fryer, instant pot, stand mixer. Each can fill half a tote. Budget high here.
  • Basement storage. Easy to forget when you're mentally walking through your home during the estimate. Open the door before you pick a package. A finished basement adds 10–15 totes minimum; a storage room with shelving can add 15–20 more.
  • Linen closets. These get counted as "one tote" but usually need three. Bedding, towels, seasonal blankets, extra pillows; they're bulky and you go through them last.
  • Kids' rooms. Toys pack inefficiently. Lego, stuffed animals, blocks, board games; they have odd shapes and dead air in every box. Budget 20–30% more than a comparable adult room.
  • The junk drawer and utility closet. Not a lot of volume, but easy to forget. Random items need a tote or two and tend to get packed last, when you've already used what you thought was your last bin.

How tote rental changes the count vs. cardboard boxes

With cardboard, you'd typically use small boxes for heavy items, medium for most things, and large for bulky soft goods. Estimating the right mix of sizes is genuinely complicated, and most people get it wrong by at least 20%.

With tote rentals, all containers are the same size: 24 × 20 × 12 inches. You adapt the packing density to the item, not the box size. This makes estimating simpler: one tote is one tote, regardless of contents. It also makes loading the truck easier, since a uniform stack is stable in a way that mixed-size boxes never quite are.

For estimating purposes, one ToteMove tote is roughly equivalent to one medium cardboard box. If an online calculator gives you a box count, use the same number as your tote starting point.

Frequently asked questions

What if I'm between package sizes?

Go up. The cost difference between ToteMove packages is $30 to $40, and running short halfway through pack day is far more disruptive than having a few extra totes sitting around. If you finish with 5 totes unused, that's fine; we pick up everything.

Can I just pack totes more densely and use fewer?

You can, but heavy totes become hard to move safely. ToteMove totes hold up to 60 lbs, but a 60-lb tote is a lot to carry up stairs. For dense items like books, dishware, or tools, fill to about 50% capacity. Keeping the count accurate means the work is faster and safer on move day.

Do moving totes count the same as cardboard boxes for estimating?

Roughly yes. A ToteMove tote (24 × 20 × 12 inches) holds about as much as a medium cardboard box. If an online calculator tells you 45 medium boxes for a 2-bedroom, 45 totes is a reliable starting point. Totes are slightly more efficient to stack and load, so you may end up needing a few fewer.

What do people most often underestimate when counting?

The kitchen and the basement, in that order. The kitchen has more stuff than any other room once you add small appliances, pantry contents, dishware, glassware, and utensils. The basement is easy to forget entirely when estimating, then you hit pack day and realize there's a full storage room still to go.
