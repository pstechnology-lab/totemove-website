Moving checklist for Orangeville, ON, 2026

By Steven, Founder  ·  October 2026  ·  Brampton, ON

Moving to Orangeville from anywhere in Peel or Halton means leaving the regional-government model behind. There's no Region handling your water and no Peel board handling your kids' school. Orangeville is in Dufferin County, the Town runs its own water system, and the public school board changes entirely.

None of that is difficult. It's just that none of the phone numbers you used last time will work. Here's the timeline. Adjust the weeks to your own possession or lease date.

8 weeks before move day

  • Decide what's coming before you book containers. Sorting first is the cheapest way to drop a package size.
  • Book your truck or movers. Longer-distance moves out of the GTA book up earlier and quote differently, so start here.
  • Book your tote rental. We deliver 7 days before move day. We bring totes and a dolly to Orangeville, and we collect from your new address after.
  • Renting? Serve 60 days written notice to vacate.
  • Buying? Confirm the possession date in writing with your lawyer.
  • Start the school conversation now if you have kids. This is a board change, not a transfer, and it's the longest lead-time item on this list.

6 weeks before move day

  • School transfers (public): Upper Grand District School Board at ugdsb.ca. Upper Grand covers Dufferin County, Wellington County, and Guelph. If you're coming from Peel, this is a different board entirely, with its own registration process and catchments.
  • School transfers (Catholic): Dufferin-Peel Catholic District School Board at dpcdsb.org. This one does carry over from Peel, since the board spans both Dufferin and Peel.
  • Request records from the current school rather than assuming they follow automatically across boards.
  • Notify your employer for payroll and T4 correspondence.
  • Set up Canada Post mail redirection.
  • Gather the documents you'll want mid-move: agreement or lease, insurance, health cards, passports.

4 weeks before move day

  • CRA address update at canada.ca/my-cra-account.
  • Driver's licence: six days after moving under Ontario rules. Book the ServiceOntario appointment now.
  • Health card and vehicle permit: same appointment.
  • Banks, investments, pension, pre-authorized payments.
  • Subscriptions and deliveries. Worth confirming which couriers and grocery services actually reach your new address, since coverage thins out north of the GTA.
  • If you commute, drive the route at your real departure time before you're committed.

2 weeks before move day: Orangeville utilities

  • Electricity and water (Orangeville Hydro): One call covers both. Orangeville Hydro is the local distributor and also handles town water billing, reachable at 519-942-8000. Open or transfer your account and set the move-out date for the old address at the same time.
  • Water and wastewater system: The Town of Orangeville runs the system itself rather than an upper-tier region. For anything beyond billing, the Town is who you call, not a region.
  • Natural gas (Enbridge Gas): Transfer or open at enbridge.com, and confirm connection if you're moving into a new build.
  • Internet and phone: Book installation now, and check what's actually available at the address rather than assuming GTA-level options.
  • Home insurance: Transfer or update. Premiums and coverage can shift once you're outside the GTA, so ask rather than assume the old policy ports cleanly.
The Peel habits that don't transfer. In Brampton and Mississauga, Region of Peel bills your water and Alectra does hydro. In Orangeville, Orangeville Hydro does both. People spend real time hunting for a separate water account that doesn't exist.

Winter moves are different up here

Orangeville sits higher and further from the lake than the rest of our service area, and it gets meaningfully worse winter weather on the same day as Brampton. A move that would be routine in Mississauga in February can be a genuinely bad day in Orangeville.

If you're moving between November and March, build in a contingency date, keep the driveway and walkway cleared for the crew, and remember that plastic totes handle snow and wet ground in a way cardboard simply doesn't. Cardboard left on a wet tailgate for ten minutes is a problem. A tote isn't.

1 week before move day

  • Your totes arrive. Start with the rooms you use least.
  • Label lids rather than sides, since lids face up when totes stack.
  • Confirm the move time, and confirm the weather forecast if it's winter.
  • Pack an essentials tote that rides in your own vehicle.
  • Empty and defrost the fridge and freezer.
  • Confirm switch-on dates at both addresses.

Move day

  • Walk the old place: closets, cupboards, garage, shed, basement.
  • Take meter readings at both ends.
  • Photograph both properties, especially if renting.
  • Keep the essentials tote with you.
  • Clear and salt the walkway if there's any chance of ice.
  • Hand over keys, remotes, and mailbox keys.

First week in your new Orangeville home

  • Confirm the Orangeville Hydro account shows the right address and start date, and that water billing is on it.
  • Check waste and recycling collection days for your street through the Town.
  • Finish the school registration if it's still open.
  • Look up Orangeville Transit routes if anyone in the house will use them.
  • Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms. Ontario requires working alarms on every storey and outside sleeping areas.
  • Unpack at a reasonable pace. You have a week before we collect the totes.

Frequently asked questions

Who provides electricity in Orangeville?

Orangeville Hydro, at 519-942-8000. Coming from Peel or Halton you're changing providers entirely, since this is neither Alectra nor Hydro One territory.

Who bills for water in Orangeville?

Orangeville Hydro, on the same account as your electricity. The Town of Orangeville runs the water and wastewater system itself rather than an upper-tier region, which is the structural difference from Peel and Halton.

Which school board serves Orangeville?

Upper Grand District School Board for public schools, covering Dufferin County, Wellington County, and Guelph. Catholic is Dufferin-Peel, which does span both counties. Coming from Peel, the public board change is the long-lead item.

Is Orangeville part of Peel Region?

No, it's in Dufferin County. The Town handles water, wastewater, and local services directly rather than through a regional government, so the numbers you used in Peel won't help.

Does Orangeville get more snow than the GTA?

Generally, yes. It sits higher and further from the lake, and conditions are routinely worse on the same day. For a winter move, build in a contingency and keep the driveway clear.
