Moving box costs vs. tote rental: what a 2-bedroom move actually costs

By Steven, Founder  ·  Updated July 2026  ·  Brampton, ON

Most people guess wrong on cardboard. You go to Home Depot, eyeball the boxes, buy what looks like enough, and drive back a second time because it wasn't. By move day you've spent more than expected, taped together boxes that still feel flimsy, and you haven't even started packing.

This post breaks down the real cost of each option for a 2-bedroom move in the GTA, with full line items, honest tradeoffs, and no spin. The number at the end might surprise you.

The cardboard cost: full line items

A 2-bedroom move needs roughly 40 to 60 boxes. At Home Depot in the GTA, boxes run $2 to $5 depending on size, call it $3 on average across a mixed order of small, medium, and large. Here's what the full cost actually looks like when you add everything:

That $280 assumes nothing breaks and you only make one extra store trip. It also doesn't include the 60 to 90 minutes you'll spend taping box bottoms before you can pack a single item, or the hour you'll spend breaking them all down and managing the disposal afterward.

The disposal line is the one most people skip entirely. In Peel Region, if your recycling bin is over capacity, you need multiple pickup cycles or a trip to a Region of Peel depot. Some homeowners pay for a bulk pickup to clear the stack. Either way, it's not zero.

Where the cardboard math goes wrong

The sticker price on boxes is only part of the cost. Here's what else accumulates without most people noticing.

The second store trip. Almost everyone underestimates how many boxes they need. One extra return trip to Home Depot costs gas, time, and the specific irritation of doing it on the weekend before a move.

Tape is a recurring expense. Three rolls sounds like a lot until you're taping 50 box bottoms, 50 box tops, and reinforcing the ones that feel soft. You'll use every roll.

The dolly problem. Home Depot boxes don't come with a dolly. U-Haul rents one for around $40. Without it, you're carrying boxes by hand for every load.

Box failure under weight. Budget cardboard gives out at the bottom seam when loaded heavy: books, kitchen contents, anything dense. If a box fails mid-carry, you're dealing with a mess and potentially damaged items on top of the cost.

The recycling pile. By the end of your move you have a stack of flattened boxes that needs to be bundled, put out, and managed across multiple recycling cycles. It's not hard, but it's another task that lands on move-out day when you're already at capacity.

What the totes are actually like to use

No assembly required

Cardboard boxes need to be folded, assembled, and taped before you can put a single item inside. Plastic totes open, fill, and snap shut. On a busy moving weekend, the difference across 45 containers is close to an hour of your life back.

They stack flush in the truck

ToteMove totes are uniform in size. They stack flat-edge-to-flat-edge, stay stable in the truck, and don't shift during transit the way mixed-size cardboard boxes do. A stable stack in the truck also means fewer items get damaged from boxes toppling mid-move.

The dolly changes the whole day

Stack three fully loaded totes on the dolly and move them to the truck in one trip. That's one trip instead of three. Multiply that over a full 2-bedroom move and you cut loading time significantly. You're also carrying less weight per trip, which matters at hour four.

They hold up in real conditions

Cardboard absorbs moisture quickly. A light rain on move-in day, a wet truck ramp, humidity in a basement storage area: any of these will weaken cardboard. Heavy-duty plastic totes are sealed when the lid snaps shut. Rain doesn't change anything. Wrap fragile items in packing paper or clothing as you normally would. The difference is the exterior protection: tote walls resist compression from other boxes stacking against them in the truck.

The hidden cost nobody talks about: your time

Cost comparisons almost always focus on dollars and ignore hours. Here's the honest time accounting for a 2-bedroom move with cardboard:

  • 30 to 45 minutes at the store, plus a second trip if you underestimated
  • 60 to 90 minutes assembling and taping box bottoms before packing begins
  • Ongoing tape use every time you close a box
  • 60 to 90 minutes breaking down and bundling boxes after the move
  • Time managing the recycling pile across one or more pickup cycles

That's three to five hours of cardboard admin on top of the move itself. Most people absorb this without naming it, but it's real time on the most exhausting weekend of the year.

With totes, that time is close to zero. Open, pack, snap shut, load onto dolly, roll to truck. After the move, schedule a pickup. Done. The question isn't just which option costs less money. When you add time, the gap between cardboard and tote rental gets larger, not smaller.

When cardboard still makes sense

Not every move favors totes. Here's where cardboard is a reasonable call:

  • Studio or bachelor move with under 15 boxes total. Low volume, low complexity, and you probably have tape around the house.
  • Wardrobe boxes for hanging clothes. Standard totes don't replicate a wardrobe box well. If you have a large walk-in closet and want to move clothes on the hanger, specialty cardboard wardrobe boxes are the right tool.
  • Specialty dish packs. Cardboard dish packs with cell dividers are purpose-built for fragile dishware. Totes handle dishes fine with proper wrapping, but if you have a lot of glassware and want a dedicated container, the dish pack exists.
  • You're moving 3 blocks in 2 hours. At that scale, whatever you already have on hand is probably fine.

For anything 2 bedrooms and up with a real moving day involved, the math doesn't favor cardboard. You pay more for something weaker that ends up in the recycling bin.
