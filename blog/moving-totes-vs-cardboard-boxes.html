Moving totes vs cardboard boxes: the real cost comparison

By Steven, Founder  ·  June 2026  ·  Brampton, ON

Most people guess wrong on cardboard. You go to Home Depot, eyeball the boxes, buy what looks like enough, and drive back a second time because it wasn't. By move day you've spent more than expected, taped together boxes that still feel flimsy, and you haven't even started packing.

Here's what a 2-bedroom move actually costs each way.

Cardboard

A 2-bedroom move needs roughly 40 to 60 boxes. At Home Depot in the GTA, boxes run $2 to $5 depending on size. Average it at $3.

ItemCost
50 mixed boxes~$150
Packing tape (3 rolls)~$25
Packing paper~$20
Dolly rental from U-Haul~$40
Total~$235

That's assuming nothing breaks. Cardboard collapses when wet, stacks unevenly, and the cheap ones give out at the bottom when you load them heavy.

There's also the time: two trips to the store, an hour taping box bottoms, another hour breaking them down afterward. Nobody adds that to the bill, but it's there.

So which is cheaper?

Cardboard costs ~$235. Tote rental costs $189. That's $46 cheaper for the rental, less than a tank of gas, and you don't have to do anything except pack.

The totes also hold their shape under weight, stack flush in a truck, and you can roll them on the included dolly instead of carrying boxes by hand. Moves go faster. The totes get cleaned and reused 400+ times rather than going to the dumpster after one move.

When cardboard makes sense

If you're moving a studio with 10 boxes and you already own tape, cardboard is fine. Wardrobe boxes and specialty dish packs are harder to replicate with standard totes. But for anything 2 bedrooms and up, the math doesn't really favor cardboard. You're paying more for something that's weaker and ends up in the recycling bin.
