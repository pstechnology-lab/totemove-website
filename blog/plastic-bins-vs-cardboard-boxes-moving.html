Are plastic bins better than cardboard boxes for moving?

By Steven, Founder  ·  June 2026  ·  Brampton, ON

You've done it before. You drive to the store, pick up a stack of cardboard boxes, bring them home, assemble each one, tape the bottoms, tape the seams, pack, tape the tops, and haul them to the truck. Then half of them are recycling by morning.

There's a better option. Reusable plastic moving bins have become the go-to for homeowners who want a faster, cleaner, less wasteful move. But are they actually better than cardboard? Let's get into it.

The quick answer

Yes. For most residential moves, heavy-duty plastic bins are the better choice. They're stronger, faster to pack, and they don't end up in a landfill. The main reason people still default to cardboard is habit — not value.

Bottom line: Plastic moving bins are weather-resistant, crush-resistant, and reusable — making them ideal for protecting household items during transport.

Where cardboard still makes sense

Cardboard has one real advantage: you can find it for free. If budget is the only factor and you're moving a small studio, picking up boxes from a grocery store works. Here's where cardboard holds up:

  • Very short, low-stakes moves with minimal furniture
  • Items you're donating and don't need to protect
  • Oddly-shaped items like lamp shades or mattresses

Outside those situations, the free-box math stops adding up fast. By the time you've bought tape, replaced collapsed boxes mid-pack, and dealt with the recycling pile, the savings are gone.

Why plastic moving totes win for most moves

No assembly. No tape.

Cardboard boxes have to be folded, assembled, and taped before you can pack a single thing. Plastic totes open, fill, and snap shut. On a busy moving weekend, the time savings alone are worth it.

Lids that actually seal

Snap-locking lids protect your belongings from dust, rain, and mid-move chaos. They also mean boxes don't pop open in the truck. Anyone who's had a box of dishes shift and open during a move knows how quickly that goes wrong.

They stack properly

Moving totes are uniform in size. They stack flat-edge-to-flat-edge, stay stable in the truck, and don't require creative Tetris packing to stop them from toppling. Standard household bins are a different story — rental totes designed specifically for moving are built to stack.

No landfill guilt

Cardboard moving boxes are single-use. Rental totes go back into rotation after your move, cleaned and ready for the next customer. Over hundreds of rental cycles, the environmental math is dramatically better.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use plastic bins if I'm hiring movers?

Yes. Professional movers handle plastic totes the same as cardboard. The uniform size actually makes loading faster and easier for the crew.

Do I need to pack items differently in plastic totes?

Not significantly. Fragile items still need wrapping. Books go in smaller quantities to keep weight manageable. The big difference is you skip tape entirely — fill, snap the lid, stack.

Are plastic totes better for a rainy moving day?

Significantly. Cardboard absorbs moisture in minutes and loses structural integrity fast. A sealed plastic tote handles rain, humidity, and a wet truck floor with no issues.
