Are plastic bins better than cardboard boxes for moving?

By Steven, Founder  ·  Updated July 2026  ·  Brampton, ON

You have done this before. Drive to a store, grab a stack of flattened cardboard boxes, bring them home, assemble each one, tape the bottoms, tape the seams, fill them, tape the tops, haul them to the truck, and by the time the move is over, half of them are crushed in your recycling bin.

Reusable plastic totes have become the go-to choice for homeowners who want a faster, cleaner, and less wasteful move. But "better" is worth unpacking. Here is a full comparison across every factor that actually matters on moving day.

The quick answer

For most residential moves, heavy-duty plastic bins outperform cardboard in durability, weather resistance, packing speed, and environmental impact. The only area where cardboard wins is upfront availability. The reason most people still reach for it is habit, not logic.

Bottom line: Plastic moving bins are weather-resistant, crush-resistant, and reusable, making them ideal for protecting household items during transport.

Where cardboard still makes sense

Cardboard has one genuine advantage: it is easy to find, sometimes for free. If budget is the only factor and you are moving a small studio apartment across the street, sourcing boxes from a grocery store works. Here is where cardboard holds up:

  • Very short, low-stakes moves with minimal furniture
  • Lightweight items you are donating and do not need to protect
  • Oddly-shaped items like lamp shades, tall mirrors, or flat-screen TV boxes where specialty cardboard shapes exist

Outside those situations, the free-box math falls apart quickly. By the time you have bought tape, replaced boxes that collapsed mid-pack, and dealt with the disposal pile afterward, the savings evaporate.

Why plastic totes win for most moves

No assembly. No tape.

Cardboard boxes have to be folded, assembled, and taped before you can pack a single item. Heavy-duty moving totes open, fill, and snap shut. On a busy moving weekend, that time difference matters more than most people expect. Multiply it across 40 or 50 containers and you recover most of a morning.

Lids that seal properly

Snap-locking lids protect your belongings from dust, rain, and mid-move chaos. They also mean totes do not pop open in the truck. Anyone who has had a box of dishes shift and open during transport knows how quickly that situation gets expensive.

They stack with confidence

Heavy-duty moving totes are uniform in size. They stack flat-edge-to-flat-edge, stay stable in the truck, and do not require creative packing strategies to keep them from toppling. Standard household plastic bins are a different story; rental totes designed specifically for moving are built to stack cleanly five totes high.

How to pack moving totes efficiently

Plastic totes change how you pack, and getting it right saves real time. These are the techniques that make the biggest difference.

Pack by room, close the lid immediately

Each tote gets one room. Label the side of the tote with a chalk marker or masking tape before you start filling. Close and snap the lid as soon as the tote is full. You will not be hunting for a box of kitchen items hiding in the living room pile at 11 PM.

Heavy on the bottom, light on top

Books, cookware, and tools go in first. Pillows, linens, and soft goods go in last. Totes stack well when the weight is distributed evenly through the column. A top-heavy tote stack is a falling tote stack.

Use the full volume

Unlike cardboard, totes do not flex or bow. Fill them close to capacity. Items will not shift once the lid snaps shut because there is no give in the walls. This is a meaningful difference when you are loading a truck and want to minimize shifting in transit.

Fragile items still need wrapping

Plastic walls protect from exterior pressure, moisture, and dust. They do not cushion items against each other. Wrap glassware, frames, and ceramics in packing paper or clothing before placing them in the tote. The same rule applies to cardboard, but the exterior protection from a sealed tote is significantly better.

Use the dolly as part of your system

The dolly that comes with every ToteMove rental is not an afterthought. Stack three fully loaded totes on the dolly and move them to the truck in one trip. That is one trip instead of three. Over the course of a full home move, that compounds into a significant reduction in time and physical effort.

Quick packing tip Pack one room entirely before starting the next. Fully loaded totes are harder to misidentify than half-packed ones. One room, one label, lid closed, on the dolly.

The real cost of cardboard you are not counting

The price tag on a stack of cardboard boxes is not the full cost. There are two disposal costs most people skip in their math.

Disposal time. Peel Region drop-off depots accept flattened cardboard at no charge, but plan for it. There are lineups at the drop-off and at the exit scales, and you need to have everything flattened and bundled before you arrive. If your recycling bin is already full after move-out, you are either waiting through multiple curbside pickup cycles or making a trip to the depot. Neither is fast.

Your time. Breaking down 40 boxes, bundling them, and managing the recycling pile takes 60 to 90 minutes most homeowners do not budget for on move-out day. That is 60 to 90 minutes you spend on cleanup instead of settling into your new place. ToteMove pickup is one phone call. We come to you.

The environmental math. Cardboard is recyclable, but recycling has a carbon cost. The collection, processing, and reconstitution of cardboard pulp still consumes energy and water. A single heavy-duty plastic tote replaces hundreds of cardboard box cycles over its lifetime. Rental totes come back, get cleaned and sanitized, and go back into service. The per-move environmental footprint of a rental tote is a fraction of the cardboard alternative.

Peel Region drop-off depots accept flattened cardboard at no charge, but expect lineups: both at the drop-off and at the exit scales. Have everything flattened and bundled before you go, and don't plan it for move-out day.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use plastic rental totes if I am hiring movers?

Yes. Professional moving crews handle plastic totes the same as cardboard. The uniform size actually makes truck loading faster and more predictable for the crew, because every tote stacks identically. Some crews prefer them.

How much weight can ToteMove totes hold?

The Round Trip Totes used in every ToteMove rental are built for real moving loads, not light household storage. The standard size (25.2" × 15.5" × 11") handles books, kitchen contents, and small appliances reliably. Keep individual tote weight under 50 lbs for comfortable one-person carrying.

Do I need to pack items differently in plastic totes than in cardboard boxes?

Fragile items still need wrapping, same as cardboard. The difference is you skip tape entirely and get significantly better exterior protection from moisture, dust, and compression. Fill to capacity, snap the lid, stack. The tote handles the rest.
